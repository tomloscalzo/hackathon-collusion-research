1. Feasibility verdict for "distilled results by this evening"

Not feasible as a full result; feasible as a first contact. The arithmetic is not the problem: tokens are cheap (a 40-run game cell is about 751k tokens [repo], 5-8 GPU-minutes on any of the GPU/model pairs considered [est], and the whole pre-declared family for one model is 1-1.5 GPU-hours, $1.5-6 [est]). The blockers are elsewhere:

  1. Modal is not yet approved. Call the approval time T. From T to the first real reply is about 60 minutes (secret, deploy, image build, weight download, cold start) [est]; every schedule below is written from T, because the 14:00 approval the first draft assumed has passed. Two gotchas to settle at T: the credit may need a payment method on file, and the GPU string in the recipe may have to be "A10" rather than "A10G" [web] (changed to "A10" with D17).
  2. The harness has never met a real model. Parse failures, guided-decoding rejections of the reply schema, verbose replies tripping the budget reservation (F17) and refusal counting (F19) are each a plausible first-hour bug. Budget 60-90 minutes for this.
  3. The honest statistical floor. At the declared 10 paraphrases × 4 seeds = 40 runs per cell, the minimum detectable effect at 80% power is about 0.25 on P(adhere) at the unadjusted one-sided level and 0.35-0.40 at the hardest Holm step [sim]; the spec itself says many predictions may come out INCONCLUSIVE at this size. A distilled result tonight would most likely read "the model can (or cannot) play the game; P1-A and P5 inconclusive at n = 40".
  4. Writing time. Analysis, bands, sanity checks and a one-page summary take 2 hours after the last run even with the pipeline in place.

3. How distillation works

Everything flows from one design CSV (cell, arm, factor levels, stage, family, predicted sign, band seats) that both the launcher and the analysis read. After a run: shards are globbed, every file is validated with check_schemas.py, manifests are checked for one model snapshot, temperature and template-hash set per model, a sample of shards is replayed from its cache, caches are merged with first-hash-wins de-duplication (a same-hash-different-text conflict is an alarm), and one tidy per-run table is built. The frozen tools/analysis.py decides the pre-declared family unchanged; a separate runner applies the same paired paraphrase-cluster bootstrap to the exploratory family under Benjamini-Hochberg at q = 0.10 with each contrast's matched null band. Read gates must all pass before any number is read: controls met per model, refusal rates plausible and balanced across paired arms, replay OK, no cache conflicts, no cap leakage in any cached prompt, paraphrase index balanced across shards and endpoints. Every finding then enters a ledger row with its statistic, interval, family status, band edge, paraphrase spread, refusal bound, n per arm, evidence pointers (shard files, cell ids, analysis JSON) and a confidence grade (A: pre-declared with band; B: exploratory, BH-significant and robust; C: suggestive; D: descriptive). An interestingness score (effect size in band units × robustness across paraphrases, refusal bound and models × a fixed relevance weight from the sprint sub-questions and the AUD-* areas) ranks the ledger; the one-page summary and the ranked next-steps list are rendered from the ledger by template, numbers only. An LLM may rewrite only the caveat and limitations prose of the numbers-filled template; summarize.py --verify-rewrite then requires every verdict row, finding line and control line to survive verbatim and every number to be in the ledger, and blocks the summary otherwise (tested on the mock: an altered verdict row is caught). Details in ANALYSIS_AND_DISTILLATION_PIPELINE.md.
