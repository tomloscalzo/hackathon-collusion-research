Interpretation
It appears that a higher P(evidence | investigation) (the chance an investigation produces a guilty finding and a fine) makes collusion rarer and gentler, because the fine becomes more likely to be paid; it therefore matters only when there is a fine. The effect is clearest in markets with no minimum price step. Across all markets it flattens at the top, which appears to come from a modelling simplification in markets with a minimum price step (an investigation that finds nothing is treated as worse for firms than a conviction).
Details

Range shown: P(evidence | investigation) from 0.1 to 1; each point averages every combination of the other eight inputs in the wide grid.

Share where collusion is chosen: all markets falls from 0.39 to 0.23; markets with no minimum price step falls from 0.52 to 0.27.

Chosen collusion intensity θ*: all markets falls from 0.26 to 0.17; markets with no minimum price step falls from 0.35 to 0.19.

Share of the variation across the whole grid explained by this input alone: 0.018 for how often, 0.012 for how hard (the rest comes from the other inputs and their combinations).

With no fine, P(evidence | investigation) has no effect in markets without a price step (share 0.741 at every level) and raises collusion in markets with a price step (the modelling simplification).
