Interpretation
It appears that more market makers make collusion both rarer and gentler, in all markets and in markets with no minimum price step. Each firm's share of the cartel profit shrinks as firms are added, while undercutting the others still wins most of the market for a round, so firms need more patience to resist cheating.
Details

Range shown: Number of market makers from 2 to 10; each point averages every combination of the other eight inputs in the wide grid.

Share where collusion is chosen: all markets falls from 0.35 to 0.19; markets with no minimum price step falls from 0.49 to 0.25.

Chosen collusion intensity θ*: all markets falls from 0.27 to 0.12; markets with no minimum price step falls from 0.35 to 0.14.

Share of the variation across the whole grid explained by this input alone: 0.016 for how often, 0.024 for how hard (the rest comes from the other inputs and their combinations).

Textbook threshold: a cartel of N firms holds roughly when patience δ ≥ 1 − 1/N (about 0.5 for 2 firms, 0.75 for 4, 0.9 for 10); preferenced volume lowers it slightly. See the Patience × Number of market makers pair chart.
