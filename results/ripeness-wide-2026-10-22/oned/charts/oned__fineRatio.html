Interpretation
It appears that a higher fine ratio (the fine as a multiple of one round of a firm's full-collusion profit) is the single strongest force against collusion, cutting both how often and how hard firms collude, with large effects even at moderate fines. It most likely works only through detection: the fine is paid only if an investigation starts and finds evidence, so fines combined with a sensitive regulator screen or frequent tips are far more effective than fines alone. The pattern is the same in markets with and without a minimum price step.
Details

Range shown: Fine ratio from 0 to 500; each point averages every combination of the other eight inputs in the wide grid.

Share where collusion is chosen: all markets falls from 0.67 to 0.05; markets with no minimum price step falls from 0.74 to 0.06.

Chosen collusion intensity θ*: all markets falls from 0.45 to 0.04; markets with no minimum price step falls from 0.50 to 0.05.

Share of the variation across the whole grid explained by this input alone: 0.225 for how often, 0.157 for how hard (the rest comes from the other inputs and their combinations).

Combined lever: in markets with no price step and no tips, collusion is chosen almost exactly when regulator screen slope × P(evidence | investigation) × fine ratio × (reservation spread ratio − 1) < 4 (98.9% of those scenarios), so the four inputs substitute for each other. With a regulator screen slope of 0 and no tips, the fine never binds.
