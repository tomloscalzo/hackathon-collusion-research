Interpretation
It appears that a larger tick size (the minimum price step) makes collusion less common and less intense. Part of this is most likely real: with a price step, ordinary competition already earns a small profit, so colluding gains less over competing, and the cartel cannot fine-tune a modest price. Part appears to come from a modelling simplification in these markets (an investigation that finds nothing is treated as worse for firms than a conviction), which makes any investigation more costly.
Details

Range shown: Tick size from 0 to 0.5; each point averages every combination of the other eight inputs in the wide grid.

Share where collusion is chosen: all markets falls from 0.38 to 0.20.

Chosen collusion intensity θ*: all markets falls from 0.26 to 0.14.

Share of the variation across the whole grid explained by this input alone: 0.027 for how often, 0.019 for how hard (the rest comes from the other inputs and their combinations).

At P(evidence | investigation) = 1, where the simplification has no effect, larger tick sizes still lower how often firms collude (for example N = 2: 0.349 → 0.279 → 0.195), so part of the effect is real.
