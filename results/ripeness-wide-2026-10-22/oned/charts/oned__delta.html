Interpretation
It appears that more patience (the chance the market continues for another round) makes collusion more common and more intense, because future cartel profits are worth protecting and the threat of a price war carries more weight. In markets with no minimum price step, how often firms collude keeps rising and then levels off, while intensity peaks at high patience and eases slightly at the very top, most likely because very patient cartels choose gentler prices to avoid investigations. Across all markets both measures drop at the highest patience; that drop appears to come from a modelling simplification in markets with a minimum price step (an investigation that finds nothing is treated as worse for firms than a conviction).
Details

Range shown: Patience from 0.5 to 0.99; each point averages every combination of the other eight inputs in the wide grid.

Share where collusion is chosen: all markets peaks at 0.37 in the middle of the range (0.17 at the lowest level, 0.27 at the highest); markets with no minimum price step rises from 0.12 to 0.51.

Chosen collusion intensity θ*: all markets peaks at 0.26 in the middle of the range (0.10 at the lowest level, 0.17 at the highest); markets with no minimum price step peaks at 0.34 in the middle of the range (0.08 at the lowest level, 0.30 at the highest).

Share of the variation across the whole grid explained by this input alone: 0.021 for how often, 0.024 for how hard (the rest comes from the other inputs and their combinations).

Markets with no price step: how often firms collude rises 0.12 → 0.51 and levels off from δ 0.95; θ* peaks at 0.34 (δ 0.95). The all-markets dip at δ 0.99 disappears when every investigation ends in a conviction (P(evidence) = 1), which confirms it is the modelling simplification.
