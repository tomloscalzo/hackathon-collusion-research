Interpretation
The reservation spread ratio (the room between cost and the spread at which customers stop trading) appears to have a small effect. In markets with no minimum price step, more room makes collusion steadily rarer and gentler, most likely because the wider spreads a cartel would charge are more visible to the regulator screen and the fine looms larger against the profit from a small markup. Across all markets both measures first rise and then fall, most likely because markets with a price step need some room above normal prices before a cartel can raise prices at all.
Details

Range shown: Reservation spread ratio from 2 to 12; each point averages every combination of the other eight inputs in the wide grid.

Share where collusion is chosen: all markets peaks at 0.33 in the middle of the range (0.24 at the lowest level, 0.28 at the highest); markets with no minimum price step falls from 0.48 to 0.29.

Chosen collusion intensity θ*: all markets peaks at 0.25 in the middle of the range (0.20 at the lowest level, 0.16 at the highest); markets with no minimum price step falls from 0.37 to 0.17.

Share of the variation across the whole grid explained by this input alone: 0.005 for how often, 0.007 for how hard (the rest comes from the other inputs and their combinations).

In markets with no price step, the reservation spread ratio acts as a multiplier on the regulator screen: slope 0.1 at s̄/s₀ 2, 0.05 at 3 and 0.025 at 5 all give the same share (0.419) and θ* (0.282).
