Interpretation
It appears that a steeper regulator screen slope (how strongly wide spreads trigger an investigation) makes collusion steadily rarer and, even more strongly, gentler. Because the screen reacts to how wide the spread is, cartels that still collude most likely lower their prices to stay below its notice, which is why intensity falls faster than how often they collude. The pattern is the same in markets with and without a minimum price step.
