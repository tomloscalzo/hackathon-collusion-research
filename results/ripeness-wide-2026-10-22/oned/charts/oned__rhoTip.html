Interpretation
It appears that a higher tip probability (the chance each round that an investigation starts from a tip, whatever the prices) makes collusion less common and less intense. Unlike the regulator screen, tips cannot be avoided by pricing more gently, so they mostly shorten the cartel's expected life; combined with a heavy enough fine and a high enough chance of a guilty finding, they appear to remove collusion altogether.
Details

Range shown: Tip probability from 0 to 0.05; each point averages every combination of the other eight inputs in the wide grid.

Share where collusion is chosen: all markets falls from 0.38 to 0.20; markets with no minimum price step falls from 0.47 to 0.28.

Chosen collusion intensity θ*: all markets falls from 0.25 to 0.14; markets with no minimum price step falls from 0.29 to 0.21.

Share of the variation across the whole grid explained by this input alone: 0.029 for how often, 0.017 for how hard (the rest comes from the other inputs and their combinations).

Hard threshold: collusion is impossible once tip probability × P(evidence | investigation) × fine ratio exceeds 1. At the ripe baseline a 5% tip probability removes collusion entirely (fig4 E).
