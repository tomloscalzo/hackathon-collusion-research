Interpretation

Whether firms collude: It appears that collusion falls as P(evidence | investigation) (the chance an investigation produces a guilty finding) rises and rises with the preferenced volume share (the share of orders split evenly between firms whatever their price), with these acting almost independently. A higher chance of a guilty finding raises the expected fine, while the preferenced volume share makes the cartel easier to hold together, so they most likely work on different decisions. The preferenced volume share helps a bit less where a guilty finding is likely, and the flattening between the highest chances of a guilty finding appears to be a modelling simplification in markets with a minimum price step (an investigation that finds nothing is treated as worse for firms than a conviction).

How hard they collude: It appears that collusion intensity (how far the chosen spread sits between the competitive and the monopoly level) falls as P(evidence | investigation) (the chance an investigation produces a guilty finding) rises, and rises with the preferenced volume share (the share of orders split evenly between firms whatever their price) at every chance of a guilty finding. The boost from the preferenced volume share looks larger where a guilty finding is unlikely, most likely because more cartels form there, so the easing of the temptation to cheat applies to more of them.

Details
Share where collusion is chosen (left chart)

Raising the preferenced volume share from 0 to 0.6 raises the share of cells where collusion is chosen from 0.26 to 0.33. Why: preferenced flow is shared whatever the price, so an undercut steals less volume and cheating pays less.

Raising the probability an investigation finds evidence from 0.1 to 1 lowers the share of cells where collusion is chosen from 0.39 to 0.23. Why: a higher chance that an investigation ends in a conviction makes the fine more likely to be paid.

Highest: 0.44 at φ = 0.6, P(evidence) = 0.1; lowest: 0.21 at φ = 0, P(evidence) = 1.

Caveat: patterns involving tick, patience or P(evidence) are partly shaped by the π_post = 0 calibration in tick markets (a recorded limitation).

Mechanism detail: P(evidence) multiplies the fine and acts on participation, while φ acts on the incentive constraint, so the two are nearly additive (φ adds 0.089 at P(evidence) = 0.1 and 0.047 at 1.0). The flattening between 0.6 and 1.0 (φ = 0: 0.226 → 0.210) comes from tick cells, which stop responding (0.199 → 0.197) under the π_post = 0 artifact; tick-free shares keep falling at every step (see the δ × P(evidence) split).

Chosen collusion intensity θ* (right chart)

Raising the preferenced volume share from 0 to 0.6 raises the mean collusion intensity θ* from 0.17 to 0.24. Why: preferenced flow is shared whatever the price, so an undercut steals less volume and cheating pays less.

Raising the probability an investigation finds evidence from 0.1 to 1 lowers the mean collusion intensity θ* from 0.26 to 0.17. Why: a higher chance that an investigation ends in a conviction makes the fine more likely to be paid.

Highest: 0.31 at φ = 0.6, P(evidence) = 0.1; lowest: 0.14 at φ = 0, P(evidence) = 1.

Caveat: patterns involving tick, patience or P(evidence) are partly shaped by the π_post = 0 calibration in tick markets (a recorded limitation).

Mechanism detail: θ* falls with P(evidence) at each φ (φ = 0.6: 0.308 → 0.189) and rises with φ in every column; φ's gain is larger at low P(evidence) (+0.084 vs +0.046). This is likely because more cells collude there, so the incentive relief from φ applies to more of them.
