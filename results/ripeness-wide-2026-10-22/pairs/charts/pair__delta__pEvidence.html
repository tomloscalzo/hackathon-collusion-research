Interpretation

Whether firms collude: It appears that collusion becomes less common as P(evidence | investigation) (the chance an investigation produces a guilty finding) rises, because the expected fine rises, and that patience (the chance the market continues another round) raises collusion up to fairly high levels. Among the most patient firms the pattern dips and then recovers as a guilty finding becomes certain. That recovery appears to come from a modelling simplification in markets with a minimum price step (an investigation that finds nothing is treated as worse for firms than a conviction), so a higher chance of a guilty finding looks less bad to firms. In markets with no minimum price step, a higher chance of a guilty finding lowers collusion steadily.

How hard they collude: It appears that collusion intensity (how far the chosen spread sits between the competitive and the monopoly level) falls as P(evidence | investigation) (the chance an investigation produces a guilty finding) rises, most likely because each extra bit of price then carries a larger expected fine; the effect looks strongest at moderate patience (the chance the market continues another round). Intensity rises then falls with patience. Among the most patient firms intensity dips and then rises again as a guilty finding becomes certain, which appears to be a modelling simplification in markets with a minimum price step (an investigation that finds nothing is treated as worse for firms than a conviction) rather than real economics.

Details
Share where collusion is chosen (left chart)

The share of cells where collusion is chosen peaks at patience = 0.9 (0.37) and is lower at both ends (0.17 at 0.5, 0.27 at 0.99). Why: patience helps up to a point (future cartel profits are worth protecting and the price-war threat bites); the decline at very high patience comes from the π_post = 0 calibration in tick markets (see Analysis).

Raising the probability an investigation finds evidence from 0.1 to 1 lowers the share of cells where collusion is chosen from 0.39 to 0.23. Why: a higher chance that an investigation ends in a conviction makes the fine more likely to be paid.

Highest: 0.49 at δ = 0.9, P(evidence) = 0.1; lowest: 0.11 at δ = 0.5, P(evidence) = 1.

The two interact: going from the lowest to the highest δ changes the value by +0.17 when P(evidence) = 1 but by +0.06 when P(evidence) = 0.1.

Caveat: patterns involving tick, patience or P(evidence) are partly shaped by the π_post = 0 calibration in tick markets (a recorded limitation).

Mechanism detail: In tick-free markets P(evidence) matters only through the expected fine P(evidence)×F, so the share falls in every row (δ = 0.99: 0.705 → 0.360). The aggregate δ = 0.99 row instead dips and recovers (0.311, 0.253, 0.227, 0.286) because tick cells there rise from 0.114 to 0.249 as P(evidence) goes from 0.1 to 1.0: under π_post = 0 a trigger without evidence is worse than a conviction (firms lose all tick rents instead of paying F and returning to π^N), and the gap grows with the horizon. That upturn is a calibration artifact, not a deterrence result.

Chosen collusion intensity θ* (right chart)

The mean collusion intensity θ* peaks at patience = 0.9 (0.26) and is lower at both ends (0.10 at 0.5, 0.17 at 0.99). Why: patience helps up to a point (future cartel profits are worth protecting and the price-war threat bites), but very patient coalitions choose lower margins to avoid the screen, and in tick markets the π_post = 0 calibration makes long horizons costly.

Raising the probability an investigation finds evidence from 0.1 to 1 lowers the mean collusion intensity θ* from 0.26 to 0.17. Why: a higher chance that an investigation ends in a conviction makes the fine more likely to be paid.

Highest: 0.34 at δ = 0.9, P(evidence) = 0.1; lowest: 0.07 at δ = 0.5, P(evidence) = 1.

Caveat: patterns involving tick, patience or P(evidence) are partly shaped by the π_post = 0 calibration in tick markets (a recorded limitation).

Mechanism detail: θ* falls with P(evidence) because the marginal expected penalty of a higher margin is (∂P(trigger)/∂θ)×P(evidence)×F; the largest fall is at δ = 0.8 (0.331 → 0.178). The U-shape at δ = 0.99 (0.193 → 0.149 → 0.189) is the same tick artifact: tick-free θ* falls monotonically there (0.402 → 0.220).
