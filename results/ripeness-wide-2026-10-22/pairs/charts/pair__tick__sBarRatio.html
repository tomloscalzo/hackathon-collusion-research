Interpretation

Whether firms collude: It appears that collusion falls with the tick size (the minimum price step) and peaks at middle values of the reservation spread ratio (the room between cost and the spread at which customers stop trading; "room" below). In markets with no price step, collusion falls as the room widens, most likely because the same degree of collusion becomes a wider spread that the regulator's screen (its rule for opening investigations when spreads are wide) notices. With a large price step and the narrowest room, collusion is impossible, because normal competition with that step already reaches the spread at which customers stop trading. As the room widens the price step becomes small relative to it and the tick sizes converge.

How hard they collude: It appears that collusion intensity (how far the chosen spread sits between the competitive and the monopoly level) falls with the tick size (the minimum price step) and peaks at a fairly low, but not the lowest, reservation spread ratio (the room between cost and the spread at which customers stop trading; "room" below). In markets with no price step, intensity falls steadily as the room widens, apparently because the regulator's screen (its rule for opening investigations when spreads are wide) sees the wider spread. With a large price step and the narrowest room there is no collusion at all, and with a smaller step the only option there is a jump straight to the full-collusion price.

Details
Share where collusion is chosen (left chart)

Raising the tick size from 0 to 0.5 lowers the share of cells where collusion is chosen from 0.38 to 0.20. Why: a coarser tick gives competitors a profit floor (tick rents), so colluding gains less over competing; in this calibration it also makes an inconclusive investigation costly (π_post = 0).

The share of cells where collusion is chosen peaks at the reservation spread ratio = 5 (0.33) and is lower at both ends (0.24 at 2, 0.28 at 12). Why: more room between cost and the customers' walk-away spread means bigger cartel profits, but the screen reacts to the absolute markup, so the wide spreads a cartel would charge in roomy markets are also more likely to be flagged.

Highest: 0.48 at tick = 0, s̄/s₀ = 2; lowest: 0.00 at tick = 0.5, s̄/s₀ = 2.

The two interact: going from the lowest to the highest tick changes the value by -0.48 when s̄/s₀ = 2 but by -0.03 when s̄/s₀ = 12.

Caveat: patterns involving tick, patience or P(evidence) are partly shaped by the π_post = 0 calibration in tick markets (a recorded limitation).

Mechanism detail: Without a tick the share falls with s̄/s₀ (0.479 → 0.289) only through the screen: the same θ is a larger absolute markup when demand reaches further, and with zero fine the tick-free share is 0.741 at every s̄/s₀. With a tick a narrow range leaves little room: with a 0.5 tick and s̄/s₀ = 2 the tick-rent Nash margin already equals the monopoly margin, so collusion is impossible (0.000), and a 0.25 tick at s̄/s₀ = 2 allows a single step. As s̄/s₀ grows the tick becomes small relative to the range and the columns converge (0.289, 0.291, 0.261 at s̄/s₀ = 12).

Chosen collusion intensity θ* (right chart)

Raising the tick size from 0 to 0.5 lowers the mean collusion intensity θ* from 0.26 to 0.14. Why: a coarser tick gives competitors a profit floor (tick rents), so colluding gains less over competing; in this calibration it also makes an inconclusive investigation costly (π_post = 0).

The mean collusion intensity θ* peaks at the reservation spread ratio = 3 (0.25) and is lower at both ends (0.20 at 2, 0.16 at 12). Why: more room between cost and the customers' walk-away spread means bigger cartel profits, but the screen reacts to the absolute markup, so the wide spreads a cartel would charge in roomy markets are also more likely to be flagged.

Highest: 0.37 at tick = 0, s̄/s₀ = 2; lowest: 0.00 at tick = 0.5, s̄/s₀ = 2.

The two interact: going from the lowest to the highest tick changes the value by -0.37 when s̄/s₀ = 2 but by -0.02 when s̄/s₀ = 12.

Caveat: patterns involving tick, patience or P(evidence) are partly shaped by the π_post = 0 calibration in tick markets (a recorded limitation).

Mechanism detail: Without a tick θ* falls from 0.370 to 0.168 with s̄/s₀ (screen scaling); with a 0.5 tick it is 0 at s̄/s₀ = 2 and 0.15–0.19 elsewhere. With a 0.25 tick at s̄/s₀ = 2, θ* equals the share (0.242) because the only collusive option is the single step to monopoly.
