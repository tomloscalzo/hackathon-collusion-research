Interpretation

Whether firms collude: It appears that collusion becomes more common as the preferenced volume share (the share of orders split evenly between firms whatever their price) grows, and that it rises with patience (the chance the market continues another round) before easing off at the most patient end. Volume that is split evenly whatever the price cannot be won by undercutting, so it most likely shrinks the reward for cheating on the cartel. That help matters most among impatient firms and almost disappears once firms are patient enough to hold the cartel together anyway. At the very highest patience the preferenced volume share even has a slightly reversed effect, which seems to come from small markets with a minimum price step and from a modelling simplification for such markets (an investigation that finds nothing is treated as worse for firms than a conviction).

How hard they collude: It appears that collusion intensity (how far the chosen spread sits between the competitive and the monopoly level) rises with the preferenced volume share (the share of orders split evenly between firms whatever their price) and follows a rise-then-fall pattern in patience (the chance the market continues another round). The preferenced volume share makes the biggest difference among impatient firms, most likely because fewer of their cartels are forced to keep prices below what they would like in order to stop members cheating. Once firms are patient, the lines for different preferenced volume shares come together, because the temptation to cheat no longer limits the price. The fall at high patience appears to reflect patient cartels choosing caution to protect their future profits, plus a modelling simplification in markets with a minimum price step (an investigation that finds nothing is treated as worse for firms than a conviction).

Details
Share where collusion is chosen (left chart)

The share of cells where collusion is chosen peaks at patience = 0.9 (0.37) and is lower at both ends (0.17 at 0.5, 0.27 at 0.99). Why: patience helps up to a point (future cartel profits are worth protecting and the price-war threat bites); the decline at very high patience comes from the π_post = 0 calibration in tick markets (see Analysis).

Raising the preferenced volume share from 0 to 0.6 raises the share of cells where collusion is chosen from 0.26 to 0.33. Why: preferenced flow is shared whatever the price, so an undercut steals less volume and cheating pays less.

Highest: 0.39 at δ = 0.8, φ = 0.6; lowest: 0.10 at δ = 0.5, φ = 0.

The two interact: going from the lowest to the highest δ changes the value by +0.17 when φ = 0 but by +0.02 when φ = 0.6.

Caveat: patterns involving tick, patience or P(evidence) are partly shaped by the π_post = 0 calibration in tick markets (a recorded limitation).

Mechanism detail: Preferenced flow is split equally whatever the price, so an undercut wins only the (1−φ) contestable part and the patience threshold falls to δ/(1−δ) ≥ (1−φ)(N−1); that is why φ matters most at δ = 0.5 (0.099 → 0.247) and hardly at all from δ = 0.9 up (0.360–0.373), where the constraint is already slack. In tick-free markets φ enters only through that constraint (prices, the screen and the fine do not depend on φ), and the column effect vanishes at high δ (tick-free shares 0.509–0.511 at δ = 0.99). The slight reversal at δ ≥ 0.95 (0.274 → 0.265 at δ = 0.99) comes from tick cells (0.156 → 0.142), likely because with N ≤ 3 more preferencing raises the static-Nash tick margin (j* rises from 1 to 2–3), shrinking the collusive range and enlarging the rent stream exposed to the π_post = 0 artifact.

Chosen collusion intensity θ* (right chart)

The mean collusion intensity θ* peaks at patience = 0.9 (0.26) and is lower at both ends (0.10 at 0.5, 0.17 at 0.99). Why: patience helps up to a point (future cartel profits are worth protecting and the price-war threat bites), but very patient coalitions choose lower margins to avoid the screen, and in tick markets the π_post = 0 calibration makes long horizons costly.

Raising the preferenced volume share from 0 to 0.6 raises the mean collusion intensity θ* from 0.17 to 0.24. Why: preferenced flow is shared whatever the price, so an undercut steals less volume and cheating pays less.

Highest: 0.29 at δ = 0.8, φ = 0.6; lowest: 0.05 at δ = 0.5, φ = 0.

The two interact: going from the lowest to the highest δ changes the value by +0.13 when φ = 0 but by +0.00 when φ = 0.6.

Caveat: patterns involving tick, patience or P(evidence) are partly shaped by the π_post = 0 calibration in tick markets (a recorded limitation).

Mechanism detail: Same incentive logic: at δ = 0.5 θ* more than triples with φ (0.046 → 0.167) because more cells pass the constraint and fewer have it capping θ below the preferred margin; in tick-free cells the contrast is starker (0.008 at φ = 0 vs 0.159 at φ = 0.6). From δ = 0.95 the columns converge (0.241–0.243, then 0.171–0.175) because the constraint no longer binds. The rise-then-fall in δ combines easier sustainability at moderate patience with margin restraint at high patience (external-risk regime) plus the tick artifact.
