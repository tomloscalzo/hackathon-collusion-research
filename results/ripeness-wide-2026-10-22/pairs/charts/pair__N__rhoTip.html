Interpretation

Whether firms collude: It appears that collusion falls as the tip probability (the chance each round that an investigation starts from a tip, whatever the price) rises and as the number of market makers (competing firms) grows. Tips seem to hit markets with many market makers proportionally harder, most likely because a tip shortens the cartel's expected life and so weakens the hold of future profits, which matters most where the temptation to cheat is already high. In small cartels, which hold together easily, tips mainly work through expected fines and a shorter life.

How hard they collude: It appears that collusion intensity (how far the chosen spread sits between the competitive and the monopoly level) falls as the tip probability (the chance each round that an investigation starts from a tip, whatever the price) and the number of market makers (competing firms) rise, and that the proportional fall from tips is larger when there are many market makers. This seems mainly to be because fewer cartels form, especially large ones; among cartels that do form, tips tend to push prices higher, since hiding helps less when a whistleblower may strike anyway.

Details
Share where collusion is chosen (left chart)

Raising the number of market makers from 2 to 10 lowers the share of cells where collusion is chosen from 0.35 to 0.19. Why: each member's share of the cartel profit is 1/N, while a one-round undercut still captures most of the contestable flow, so the temptation to cheat grows with N.

Raising the tip probability from 0 to 0.05 lowers the share of cells where collusion is chosen from 0.38 to 0.20. Why: tips end cartels at any margin, so they shorten the expected life of the cartel.

Highest: 0.45 at N = 2, ρ_tip = 0; lowest: 0.11 at N = 10, ρ_tip = 0.05.

Mechanism detail: A tip ends the cartel whatever the margin, so it cuts effective patience to δ(1−ρ_T), which bites hardest where the incentive constraint is tight: the 5% tip lowers the share by 46% at N = 2 (0.452 → 0.243) but 60% at N = 10 (0.272 → 0.108). The share of N = 10 cells where collusion is sustainable falls from 0.546 to 0.479 with the tip, while N = 2 barely moves (0.917 → 0.915). The rest of the tip effect, expected fines and shorter tenure, does not depend on N.

Chosen collusion intensity θ* (right chart)

Raising the number of market makers from 2 to 10 lowers the mean collusion intensity θ* from 0.27 to 0.12. Why: each member's share of the cartel profit is 1/N, while a one-round undercut still captures most of the contestable flow, so the temptation to cheat grows with N.

Raising the tip probability from 0 to 0.05 lowers the mean collusion intensity θ* from 0.25 to 0.14. Why: tips end cartels at any margin, so they shorten the expected life of the cartel.

Highest: 0.33 at N = 2, ρ_tip = 0; lowest: 0.06 at N = 10, ρ_tip = 0.05.

Mechanism detail: θ* falls with the tip (N = 2: 0.326 → 0.198; N = 10: 0.157 → 0.065), mostly because fewer cells collude; given participation a tip raises the chosen margin (see the δ × ρ_tip split). The proportional fall is larger at N = 10 (−59% vs −39%) for the same incentive-constraint reason.
