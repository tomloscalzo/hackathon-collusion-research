Interpretation

Whether firms collude: It appears that a steeper regulator screen slope (how strongly the regulator's screen, its rule for opening investigations, reacts to wide spreads) sharply reduces collusion at every level of patience (the chance the market continues another round), making it the main deterrent in this chart. Patience raises the share up to fairly high levels, because the threat of a price war then holds the cartel together, and the curve for each screen slope peaks there. The drop at the most patient end seems to come from a modelling simplification in markets with a minimum price step (an investigation that finds nothing is treated as worse for firms than a conviction), which matters because patient firms value highly the ordinary earnings such an investigation destroys; without a price step the share simply levels off.

How hard they collude: It appears that collusion intensity (how far the chosen spread sits between the competitive and the monopoly level) drops steeply as the regulator screen slope (how strongly the regulator's screen, its rule for opening investigations, reacts to wide spreads) rises, and that it rises then falls with patience (the chance the market continues another round). With no screen, colluding firms most likely go all the way to the full-collusion price, because a higher price carries no extra risk of investigation. With any screen, every extra bit of price raises the chance of an investigation that would end the cartel, and very patient cartels, having the most future profit at stake, appear to hold prices down further. Even a gentle screen slope seems to bite hard on how far prices are pushed.

Details
Share where collusion is chosen (left chart)

The share of cells where collusion is chosen peaks at patience = 0.9 (0.37) and is lower at both ends (0.17 at 0.5, 0.27 at 0.99). Why: patience helps up to a point (future cartel profits are worth protecting and the price-war threat bites); the decline at very high patience comes from the π_post = 0 calibration in tick markets (see Analysis).

Raising the screen slope from 0 to 0.2 lowers the share of cells where collusion is chosen from 0.55 to 0.14. Why: a steeper screen makes high spreads more likely to trigger an investigation, so cartels must keep margins low or not collude.

Highest: 0.68 at δ = 0.95, slope = 0; lowest: 0.10 at δ = 0.5, slope = 0.2.

The two interact: going from the lowest to the highest δ changes the value by +0.33 when slope = 0 but by +0.03 when slope = 0.2.

Caveat: patterns involving tick, patience or P(evidence) are partly shaped by the π_post = 0 calibration in tick markets (a recorded limitation).

Mechanism detail: The screen is the main deterrent: raising the slope from 0 to 0.2 cuts the share by roughly two-thirds to four-fifths in every row (δ = 0.9: 0.670 → 0.169), and each slope column peaks at δ = 0.8–0.95, once patience has made the incentive constraint slack. The drop at δ = 0.99 (slope 0.025: 0.424 → 0.290 from δ = 0.95) comes from tick cells: in tick-free markets the sign of the collusion gain, π_c − ρ_E·F, does not involve δ, so once the constraint holds the share stops moving (0.570 vs 0.575 at slope 0.025). In tick markets every screen trigger without evidence destroys the π^N/(1−δ) tick-rent stream under π_post = 0, a loss that grows with δ.

Chosen collusion intensity θ* (right chart)

The mean collusion intensity θ* peaks at patience = 0.9 (0.26) and is lower at both ends (0.10 at 0.5, 0.17 at 0.99). Why: patience helps up to a point (future cartel profits are worth protecting and the price-war threat bites), but very patient coalitions choose lower margins to avoid the screen, and in tick markets the π_post = 0 calibration makes long horizons costly.

Raising the screen slope from 0 to 0.2 lowers the mean collusion intensity θ* from 0.53 to 0.06. Why: a steeper screen makes high spreads more likely to trigger an investigation, so cartels must keep margins low or not collude.

Highest: 0.68 at δ = 0.95, slope = 0; lowest: 0.04 at δ = 0.99, slope = 0.2.

The two interact: going from the lowest to the highest δ changes the value by +0.40 when slope = 0 but by -0.00 when slope = 0.2.

Caveat: patterns involving tick, patience or P(evidence) are partly shaped by the π_post = 0 calibration in tick markets (a recorded limitation).

Mechanism detail: With no screen the hazard does not depend on the margin, so colluders go straight to monopoly (in tick-free cells θ* equals the share, 0.792 at δ = 0.99), so slope 0 is the highest column in every row, peaking at 0.676 at δ = 0.95. With any screen θ* is interior and falls with patience past δ ≈ 0.9: tick-free θ* at slope 0.01 is 0.529 at δ = 0.95 and 0.464 at 0.99, the genuine external-risk effect, because each extra unit of markup raises the hazard of losing rents that last 1/(1−δ) rounds. Even a gentle screen bites hard: slope 0.01 cuts θ* at δ = 0.9 from 0.664 to 0.433.
