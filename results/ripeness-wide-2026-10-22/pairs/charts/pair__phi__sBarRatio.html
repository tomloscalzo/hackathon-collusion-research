Interpretation

Whether firms collude: It appears that collusion peaks at middle values of the reservation spread ratio (the room between cost and the spread at which customers stop trading; "room" below), and that the preferenced volume share (the share of orders split evenly between firms whatever their price) raises it by a similar amount at every level of room. This fits the preferenced volume share acting only on the temptation to cheat, while the room acts on how visible collusion is to the regulator's screen (its rule for opening investigations when spreads are wide) and, in markets with a minimum price step, on how many price levels fit above normal competition. In markets with no minimum price step, collusion most likely falls steadily with room; the rise from the narrowest room comes from markets with a price step needing space above normal competitive prices.

How hard they collude: It appears that collusion intensity (how far the chosen spread sits between the competitive and the monopoly level) peaks at a fairly low, but not the lowest, reservation spread ratio (the room between cost and the spread at which customers stop trading; "room" below) for every preferenced volume share (the share of orders split evenly between firms whatever their price), and that the preferenced volume share adds a similar amount throughout. The decline at wider room seems to be because the same degree of collusion becomes a wider spread that the regulator's screen (its rule for opening investigations when spreads are wide) notices, while the low value at the narrowest room most likely reflects markets with a minimum price step that leave little or no space to raise prices.

Details
Share where collusion is chosen (left chart)

Raising the preferenced volume share from 0 to 0.6 raises the share of cells where collusion is chosen from 0.26 to 0.33. Why: preferenced flow is shared whatever the price, so an undercut steals less volume and cheating pays less.

The share of cells where collusion is chosen peaks at the reservation spread ratio = 5 (0.33) and is lower at both ends (0.24 at 2, 0.28 at 12). Why: more room between cost and the customers' walk-away spread means bigger cartel profits, but the screen reacts to the absolute markup, so the wide spreads a cartel would charge in roomy markets are also more likely to be flagged.

Highest: 0.36 at φ = 0.6, s̄/s₀ = 5; lowest: 0.21 at φ = 0, s̄/s₀ = 2.

Mechanism detail: φ raises the share by a similar 0.05–0.08 at every demand range (s̄/s₀ = 2: 0.209 → 0.274; 12: 0.254 → 0.306), consistent with φ working only on the incentive constraint while s̄/s₀ works on screen visibility and tick room. In tick-free cells the share falls monotonically with s̄/s₀ (φ = 0.6: 0.545 → 0.331); the aggregate rise from 2 to 3–5 comes from tick cells, which have little or no room above the tick-rent Nash margin at narrow ranges.

Chosen collusion intensity θ* (right chart)

Raising the preferenced volume share from 0 to 0.6 raises the mean collusion intensity θ* from 0.17 to 0.24. Why: preferenced flow is shared whatever the price, so an undercut steals less volume and cheating pays less.

The mean collusion intensity θ* peaks at the reservation spread ratio = 3 (0.25) and is lower at both ends (0.20 at 2, 0.16 at 12). Why: more room between cost and the customers' walk-away spread means bigger cartel profits, but the screen reacts to the absolute markup, so the wide spreads a cartel would charge in roomy markets are also more likely to be flagged.

Highest: 0.29 at φ = 0.6, s̄/s₀ = 3; lowest: 0.14 at φ = 0, s̄/s₀ = 12.

Mechanism detail: θ* peaks at s̄/s₀ = 3 for every φ (φ = 0.6: 0.287); the decline beyond 3 is screen scaling (the same θ is a larger absolute markup and so a higher hazard), and the low value at 2 is the tick-room effect. φ adds 0.05–0.07 throughout.
