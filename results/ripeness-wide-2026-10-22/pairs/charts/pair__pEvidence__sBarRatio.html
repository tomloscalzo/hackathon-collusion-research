Interpretation

Whether firms collude: It appears that collusion falls as P(evidence | investigation) (the chance an investigation produces a guilty finding) rises at every reservation spread ratio (the room between cost and the spread at which customers stop trading; "room" below), and peaks at middle values of room; the peak shifts slightly towards narrower room when a guilty finding is certain. In markets with no minimum price step collusion most likely falls steadily as the room widens, because a wider room means a spread more visible to the regulator's screen (its rule for opening investigations when spreads are wide) and a heavier fine relative to the profit from a small markup. The rise from the narrowest room appears to come from markets with a price step needing space above normal prices.

How hard they collude: It appears that collusion intensity (how far the chosen spread sits between the competitive and the monopoly level) peaks at a fairly low, but not the lowest, reservation spread ratio (the room between cost and the spread at which customers stop trading; "room" below) at every level of P(evidence | investigation) (the chance an investigation produces a guilty finding), and falls as that chance rises. In markets with no minimum price step, intensity seems to fall steadily in both directions: a higher chance of a guilty finding raises the expected fine, and a wider room makes the same collusion more visible to the regulator's screen (its rule for opening investigations when spreads are wide).

Details
Share where collusion is chosen (left chart)

Raising the probability an investigation finds evidence from 0.1 to 1 lowers the share of cells where collusion is chosen from 0.39 to 0.23. Why: a higher chance that an investigation ends in a conviction makes the fine more likely to be paid.

The share of cells where collusion is chosen peaks at the reservation spread ratio = 5 (0.33) and is lower at both ends (0.24 at 2, 0.28 at 12). Why: more room between cost and the customers' walk-away spread means bigger cartel profits, but the screen reacts to the absolute markup, so the wide spreads a cartel would charge in roomy markets are also more likely to be flagged.

Highest: 0.44 at P(evidence) = 0.1, s̄/s₀ = 5; lowest: 0.20 at P(evidence) = 1, s̄/s₀ = 2.

Caveat: patterns involving tick, patience or P(evidence) are partly shaped by the π_post = 0 calibration in tick markets (a recorded limitation).

Mechanism detail: P(evidence) shifts the curve down at every s̄/s₀ (s̄/s₀ = 5: 0.439 → 0.259) and the inverted U survives (peak at 5 for P(evidence) ≤ 0.6, at 3 for 1.0). In tick-free cells the share falls monotonically with s̄/s₀ at every P(evidence) (0.1: 0.616 → 0.427), because the expected fine of a small markup, relative to its rent, scales with slope × P(evidence) × fineRatio × (s̄/s₀ − 1); the rise at the narrow end is tick room.

Chosen collusion intensity θ* (right chart)

Raising the probability an investigation finds evidence from 0.1 to 1 lowers the mean collusion intensity θ* from 0.26 to 0.17. Why: a higher chance that an investigation ends in a conviction makes the fine more likely to be paid.

The mean collusion intensity θ* peaks at the reservation spread ratio = 3 (0.25) and is lower at both ends (0.20 at 2, 0.16 at 12). Why: more room between cost and the customers' walk-away spread means bigger cartel profits, but the screen reacts to the absolute markup, so the wide spreads a cartel would charge in roomy markets are also more likely to be flagged.

Highest: 0.31 at P(evidence) = 0.1, s̄/s₀ = 3; lowest: 0.13 at P(evidence) = 1, s̄/s₀ = 12.

Caveat: patterns involving tick, patience or P(evidence) are partly shaped by the π_post = 0 calibration in tick markets (a recorded limitation).

Mechanism detail: θ* peaks at s̄/s₀ = 3 for every P(evidence) and falls with P(evidence) everywhere (s̄/s₀ = 3: 0.315 → 0.210). Tick-free θ* falls monotonically in both directions (0.486 at P(evidence) 0.1, s̄/s₀ = 2; 0.119 at 1.0, 12).
