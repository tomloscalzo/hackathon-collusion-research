Interpretation

Whether firms collude: It appears that collusion falls as the number of market makers (competing firms) grows and as P(evidence | investigation) (the chance an investigation produces a guilty finding) rises, and these act almost independently. This is most likely because a higher chance of a guilty finding raises the expected fine but does not change the temptation to cheat, which is how the number of market makers acts. The smallest numbers of market makers look almost identical, and the curve flattens as a guilty finding becomes certain, which appears to be a modelling simplification in markets with a minimum price step (an investigation that finds nothing is treated as worse for firms than a conviction).

How hard they collude: It appears that collusion intensity (how far the chosen spread sits between the competitive and the monopoly level) falls as P(evidence | investigation) (the chance an investigation produces a guilty finding) rises at every number of market makers (competing firms), and is lower with more firms. The proportional fall is smaller when there are many market makers, most likely because fewer large cartels collude at all, leaving less for deterrence to remove. The near-flat stretch at the highest chances of a guilty finding in markets with many market makers appears to be a modelling simplification in markets with a minimum price step (an investigation that finds nothing is treated as worse for firms than a conviction) offsetting the extra deterrence.

Details
Share where collusion is chosen (left chart)

Raising the number of market makers from 2 to 10 lowers the share of cells where collusion is chosen from 0.35 to 0.19. Why: each member's share of the cartel profit is 1/N, while a one-round undercut still captures most of the contestable flow, so the temptation to cheat grows with N.

Raising the probability an investigation finds evidence from 0.1 to 1 lowers the share of cells where collusion is chosen from 0.39 to 0.23. Why: a higher chance that an investigation ends in a conviction makes the fine more likely to be paid.

Highest: 0.47 at N = 3, P(evidence) = 0.1; lowest: 0.16 at N = 10, P(evidence) = 1.

The two interact: going from the lowest to the highest N changes the value by -0.21 when P(evidence) = 0.1 but by -0.11 when P(evidence) = 1.

Caveat: patterns involving tick, patience or P(evidence) are partly shaped by the π_post = 0 calibration in tick markets (a recorded limitation).

Mechanism detail: P(evidence) enters only the expected penalty, not the cheating condition (the incentive constraint depends on the trigger hazard, not on conviction), so N and P(evidence) are nearly separable: in tick-free cells the N = 10 to N = 2 ratio is about 0.50 at every level (0.338/0.681 at 0.1, 0.177/0.349 at 1.0). The flattening between 0.6 and 1.0 (N = 2: 0.297 → 0.274) comes from tick cells, which stop falling (N = 2: 0.236 → 0.237) under the π_post = 0 artifact. N = 2 and 3 are nearly identical (0.465 vs 0.466 at 0.1) because with two or three firms the constraint is slack in most cells.

Chosen collusion intensity θ* (right chart)

Raising the number of market makers from 2 to 10 lowers the mean collusion intensity θ* from 0.27 to 0.12. Why: each member's share of the cartel profit is 1/N, while a one-round undercut still captures most of the contestable flow, so the temptation to cheat grows with N.

Raising the probability an investigation finds evidence from 0.1 to 1 lowers the mean collusion intensity θ* from 0.26 to 0.17. Why: a higher chance that an investigation ends in a conviction makes the fine more likely to be paid.

Highest: 0.35 at N = 2, P(evidence) = 0.1; lowest: 0.10 at N = 10, P(evidence) = 1.

Caveat: patterns involving tick, patience or P(evidence) are partly shaped by the π_post = 0 calibration in tick markets (a recorded limitation).

Mechanism detail: θ* falls with P(evidence) at every N because the marginal expected penalty of more markup is proportional to P(evidence)×F; the relative fall is smaller at N = 10 (−29%, 0.142 → 0.101) than at N = 2 (−39%), likely because fewer N = 10 cells collude at all, leaving less to deter. The near-flat stretch from 0.6 to 1.0 at N = 6–10 (0.148 → 0.141; 0.102 → 0.101) is the tick artifact offsetting deterrence.
