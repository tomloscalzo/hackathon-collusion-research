Interpretation

Whether firms collude: It appears that collusion falls with both the tick size (the minimum price step) and the regulator screen slope (how strongly the regulator's screen, its rule for opening investigations, reacts to wide spreads), and that they reinforce each other. With no screen a small price step makes little difference, but as the screen steepens the markets with a price step fall much faster. Without a price step, a cartel can most likely choose a tiny markup that the screen barely notices; with a step, the smallest markup is a full step that the screen always sees. This gap survives when every investigation is made to end in a conviction, so it appears to be mainly genuine, with a modelling simplification in markets with a minimum price step (an investigation that finds nothing is treated as worse for firms than a conviction) adding to it.

How hard they collude: It appears that collusion intensity (how far the chosen spread sits between the competitive and the monopoly level) falls with both the tick size (the minimum price step) and the regulator screen slope (how strongly the regulator's screen, its rule for opening investigations, reacts to wide spreads). For a small price step, most of the shortfall compared with no step seems to be a modelling simplification in markets with a minimum price step (an investigation that finds nothing is treated as worse for firms than a conviction), because it vanishes when every investigation ends in a conviction. For a large price step, intensity stays lower even then, most likely because whole steps stop the cartel from choosing a modest price that would escape the screen.

Details
Share where collusion is chosen (left chart)

Raising the tick size from 0 to 0.5 lowers the share of cells where collusion is chosen from 0.38 to 0.20. Why: a coarser tick gives competitors a profit floor (tick rents), so colluding gains less over competing; in this calibration it also makes an inconclusive investigation costly (π_post = 0).

Raising the screen slope from 0 to 0.2 lowers the share of cells where collusion is chosen from 0.55 to 0.14. Why: a steeper screen makes high spreads more likely to trigger an investigation, so cartels must keep margins low or not collude.

Highest: 0.60 at tick = 0.25, slope = 0; lowest: 0.06 at tick = 0.5, slope = 0.2.

Caveat: patterns involving tick, patience or P(evidence) are partly shaped by the π_post = 0 calibration in tick markets (a recorded limitation).

Mechanism detail: With no screen a 0.25 tick is harmless (0.602 vs 0.593 without), but as the screen steepens the tick columns fall much faster (slope 0.2: 0.241 without a tick vs 0.128 and 0.064). Without a tick a cartel can pick a tiny markup whose screen hazard is near zero; with a tick the smallest collusive step is one full tick of markup, so a hazard of slope × tick cannot be avoided. The gap persists at P(evidence) = 1 with no tip, where the π_post artifact is off (slope 0.2: 0.156 vs 0.127 vs 0.084), so this granularity is likely the main driver, with the artifact adding to it in the full average.

Chosen collusion intensity θ* (right chart)

Raising the tick size from 0 to 0.5 lowers the mean collusion intensity θ* from 0.26 to 0.14. Why: a coarser tick gives competitors a profit floor (tick rents), so colluding gains less over competing; in this calibration it also makes an inconclusive investigation costly (π_post = 0).

Raising the screen slope from 0 to 0.2 lowers the mean collusion intensity θ* from 0.53 to 0.06. Why: a steeper screen makes high spreads more likely to trigger an investigation, so cartels must keep margins low or not collude.

Highest: 0.59 at tick = 0, slope = 0; lowest: 0.03 at tick = 0.5, slope = 0.2.

Caveat: patterns involving tick, patience or P(evidence) are partly shaped by the π_post = 0 calibration in tick markets (a recorded limitation).

Mechanism detail: At slope 0 θ* is 0.593/0.553/0.430 by tick and at slope 0.2 it is 0.100/0.056/0.028. With P(evidence) = 1 and no tip, the 0.25 tick matches or slightly exceeds no tick at every slope (0.060 vs 0.059 at slope 0.2) while the 0.5 tick stays lower (0.042), so for θ* the 0.25-tick shortfall in the full average is mostly the artifact, and the 0.5-tick shortfall is granularity.
