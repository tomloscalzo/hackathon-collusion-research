Interpretation

Whether firms collude: It appears that collusion falls steeply as the fine ratio (the fine as a multiple of a firm's full-collusion profit for a round) rises and as the tick size (the minimum price step) grows, and that fines bite much harder in markets with a price step. With no fine, a small price step makes little difference. In markets with no price step, a cartel can most likely pick a markup so small that the expected fine stays below its extra profit; with a price step, the smallest markup is a full step, so the fine cannot be watered down that way.

How hard they collude: It appears that collusion intensity (how far the chosen spread sits between the competitive and the monopoly level) falls as the fine ratio (the fine as a multiple of a firm's full-collusion profit for a round) rises at every tick size (the minimum price step), and falls faster in markets with a price step at low and middle fine ratios. Under very high fine ratios the tick sizes converge, most likely because the cartels that remain are mostly in scenarios where no investigation can ever start, which never face a fine and so are unaffected by the limits a price step imposes.

Details
Share where collusion is chosen (left chart)

Raising the tick size from 0 to 0.5 lowers the share of cells where collusion is chosen from 0.38 to 0.20. Why: a coarser tick gives competitors a profit floor (tick rents), so colluding gains less over competing; in this calibration it also makes an inconclusive investigation costly (π_post = 0).

Raising the fine ratio from 0 to 500 lowers the share of cells where collusion is chosen from 0.67 to 0.05. Why: bigger fines raise the expected cost of being convicted.

Highest: 0.74 at tick = 0.25, fine ratio = 0; lowest: 0.03 at tick = 0.5, fine ratio = 500.

The two interact: going from the lowest to the highest tick changes the value by -0.20 when fine ratio = 0 but by -0.03 when fine ratio = 500.

Caveat: patterns involving tick, patience or P(evidence) are partly shaped by the π_post = 0 calibration in tick markets (a recorded limitation).

Mechanism detail: At zero fine a 0.25 tick is neutral (0.744 vs 0.741: its easing of the incentive constraint offsets the artifact), but fines bite much harder with a tick: at fine 12.5 the share is 0.560 without a tick, 0.413 and 0.252 with. Without a tick a cartel can choose a markup small enough that the expected fine (screen hazard × P(evidence) × F, proportional to the markup) stays below its rent; in tick-free cells with no tip, collusion occurs iff it is sustainable and slope × P(evidence) × fineRatio × (s̄/s₀ − 1) < 4 (true in 98.9% of those cells, the rest knife-edge cells with θ* < 0.001). With a tick the smallest markup is a whole tick, so the fine cannot be diluted that way.

Chosen collusion intensity θ* (right chart)

Raising the tick size from 0 to 0.5 lowers the mean collusion intensity θ* from 0.26 to 0.14. Why: a coarser tick gives competitors a profit floor (tick rents), so colluding gains less over competing; in this calibration it also makes an inconclusive investigation costly (π_post = 0).

Raising the fine ratio from 0 to 500 lowers the mean collusion intensity θ* from 0.45 to 0.04. Why: bigger fines raise the expected cost of being convicted.

Highest: 0.50 at tick = 0, fine ratio = 0; lowest: 0.03 at tick = 0.5, fine ratio = 500.

The two interact: going from the lowest to the highest tick changes the value by -0.13 when fine ratio = 0 but by -0.02 when fine ratio = 500.

Caveat: patterns involving tick, patience or P(evidence) are partly shaped by the π_post = 0 calibration in tick markets (a recorded limitation).

Mechanism detail: θ* falls with the fine in every column (tick 0: 0.496 → 0.050), faster with a tick at low-to-mid fines (fine 25: 0.290, 0.202, 0.124). At very high fines the columns converge (0.031–0.050 at 500×) because the remaining colluders are mostly cells with no screen and no tip (68% of colluding cells at 500×), which never pay the fine.
