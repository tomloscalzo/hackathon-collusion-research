Interpretation

Whether firms collude: It appears that a higher tip probability (the chance each round that an investigation starts from a tip, whatever the price) lowers collusion at every reservation spread ratio (the room between cost and the spread at which customers stop trading; "room" below), proportionally more when the room is narrow, and that collusion peaks at middle values of room. Tips most likely matter more when the room is narrow because the regulator's screen (its rule for opening investigations when spreads are wide) sees only a small spread there, so a tip becomes the main way an investigation starts. The middle peak seems to blend a falling pattern in markets with no minimum price step, where a wider room is more visible to the screen, with markets that have a price step needing space above normal prices.

How hard they collude: It appears that collusion intensity (how far the chosen spread sits between the competitive and the monopoly level) peaks at a fairly low, but not the lowest, reservation spread ratio (the room between cost and the spread at which customers stop trading; "room" below) at every tip probability (the chance each round that an investigation starts from a tip, whatever the price), and that tips lower it throughout. Beyond the peak, a wider room most likely makes collusion more visible to the regulator's screen (its rule for opening investigations when spreads are wide), while tips seem to lower the average mainly by reducing how many cartels form.

Details
Share where collusion is chosen (left chart)

Raising the tip probability from 0 to 0.05 lowers the share of cells where collusion is chosen from 0.38 to 0.20. Why: tips end cartels at any margin, so they shorten the expected life of the cartel.

The share of cells where collusion is chosen peaks at the reservation spread ratio = 5 (0.33) and is lower at both ends (0.24 at 2, 0.28 at 12). Why: more room between cost and the customers' walk-away spread means bigger cartel profits, but the screen reacts to the absolute markup, so the wide spreads a cartel would charge in roomy markets are also more likely to be flagged.

Highest: 0.43 at ρ_tip = 0, s̄/s₀ = 3; lowest: 0.14 at ρ_tip = 0.05, s̄/s₀ = 2.

Mechanism detail: The tip shifts the curve down at every s̄/s₀ (s̄/s₀ = 3: 0.433 → 0.196), proportionally more at narrow ranges (−58% at 2, −41% at 12), likely because there the screen sees only a small absolute markup, so the tip is the main trigger. In tick-free cells the share falls with s̄/s₀ at every tip level (no tip: 0.614 → 0.346) by screen scaling; the inverted U in the full table comes from tick cells needing room above the tick-rent margin.

Chosen collusion intensity θ* (right chart)

Raising the tip probability from 0 to 0.05 lowers the mean collusion intensity θ* from 0.25 to 0.14. Why: tips end cartels at any margin, so they shorten the expected life of the cartel.

The mean collusion intensity θ* peaks at the reservation spread ratio = 3 (0.25) and is lower at both ends (0.20 at 2, 0.16 at 12). Why: more room between cost and the customers' walk-away spread means bigger cartel profits, but the screen reacts to the absolute markup, so the wide spreads a cartel would charge in roomy markets are also more likely to be flagged.

Highest: 0.32 at ρ_tip = 0, s̄/s₀ = 3; lowest: 0.12 at ρ_tip = 0.05, s̄/s₀ = 12.

Mechanism detail: θ* peaks at s̄/s₀ = 3 at every tip level (0.323, 0.259, 0.161). Beyond that greater visibility of the markup to the screen lowers it, and the tip lowers it throughout by cutting participation.
