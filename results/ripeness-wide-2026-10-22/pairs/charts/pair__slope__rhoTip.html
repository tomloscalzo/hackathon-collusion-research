Interpretation

Whether firms collude: It appears that collusion falls as the regulator screen slope (how strongly the regulator's screen, its rule for opening investigations, reacts to wide spreads) and the tip probability (the chance each round that an investigation starts from a tip, whatever the price) rise, but these act as substitutes. Tips do most damage where there is no screen and little where a steep screen already dominates, most likely because either is enough to start an investigation that ends the cartel. With no screen and no tips nothing can start an investigation, so the chart there shows simply where the cartel can be held together.

How hard they collude: It appears that average collusion intensity (how far the chosen spread sits between the competitive and the monopoly level) falls with both the regulator screen slope (how strongly the regulator's screen, its rule for opening investigations, reacts to wide spreads) and the tip probability (the chance each round that an investigation starts from a tip, whatever the price). Where there is a screen, tips seem to push the cartels that still form towards higher prices, because hiding from the screen helps little when a whistleblower may expose them anyway; the average still falls because fewer cartels form. With no screen, colluding firms most likely go straight to the full-collusion price, so tips lower intensity only by reducing how many cartels form.

Details
Share where collusion is chosen (left chart)

Raising the screen slope from 0 to 0.2 lowers the share of cells where collusion is chosen from 0.55 to 0.14. Why: a steeper screen makes high spreads more likely to trigger an investigation, so cartels must keep margins low or not collude.

Raising the tip probability from 0 to 0.05 lowers the share of cells where collusion is chosen from 0.38 to 0.20. Why: tips end cartels at any margin, so they shorten the expected life of the cartel.

Highest: 0.79 at slope = 0, ρ_tip = 0; lowest: 0.12 at slope = 0.2, ρ_tip = 0.05.

The two interact: going from the lowest to the highest slope changes the value by -0.62 when ρ_tip = 0 but by -0.19 when ρ_tip = 0.05.

Mechanism detail: The two triggers are substitutes, since P(trigger) = 1 − (1 − ρ_screen)(1 − ρ_tip): a tip does the most damage where there is no screen (slope 0: 0.787 → 0.310) and little where the screen already dominates (slope 0.2: 0.166 → 0.117). The slope-0, no-tip cell is the no-hazard case: nothing can trigger, the fine is never paid, and 0.787 is simply the share of cells where collusion is sustainable.

Chosen collusion intensity θ* (right chart)

Raising the screen slope from 0 to 0.2 lowers the mean collusion intensity θ* from 0.53 to 0.06. Why: a steeper screen makes high spreads more likely to trigger an investigation, so cartels must keep margins low or not collude.

Raising the tip probability from 0 to 0.05 lowers the mean collusion intensity θ* from 0.25 to 0.14. Why: tips end cartels at any margin, so they shorten the expected life of the cartel.

Highest: 0.75 at slope = 0, ρ_tip = 0; lowest: 0.06 at slope = 0.2, ρ_tip = 0.05.

The two interact: going from the lowest to the highest slope changes the value by -0.68 when ρ_tip = 0 but by -0.24 when ρ_tip = 0.05.

Mechanism detail: Given participation, a tip raises the chosen margin wherever there is a screen (tick-free colluders at slope 0.01: 0.703 → 0.897), because a cartel that may be caught by a tip anyway gains less from hiding; mean θ* still falls with the tip because fewer cells collude. At slope 0 the hazard does not depend on θ, so tick-free colluders all choose monopoly (θ* given participation = 1.000), and θ* drops from 0.748 to 0.293 only through participation.
