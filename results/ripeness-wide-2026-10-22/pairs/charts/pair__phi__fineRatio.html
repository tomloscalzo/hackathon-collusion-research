Interpretation

Whether firms collude: It appears that collusion falls steeply as the fine ratio (the fine as a multiple of a firm's full-collusion profit for a round) rises and rises with the preferenced volume share (the share of orders split evenly between firms whatever their price), by a broadly similar proportion at every fine ratio. The fine seems to decide whether colluding is worth the risk, while the preferenced volume share decides whether the cartel can be held together. With no fine, the chart mostly shows whether firms can keep the cartel from breaking apart, which is where the preferenced volume share matters most visibly.

How hard they collude: It appears that collusion intensity (how far the chosen spread sits between the competitive and the monopoly level) falls as the fine ratio (the fine as a multiple of a firm's full-collusion profit for a round) rises at every preferenced volume share (the share of orders split evenly between firms whatever their price), and that the advantage from the preferenced volume share shrinks to almost nothing under high fine ratios. This is most likely because heavy fines leave few cartels, mostly in scenarios where the regulator's screen (its rule for opening investigations when spreads are wide) is switched off and tips (investigations that start at random, whatever the price) never happen, where the preferenced volume share has little left to change.

Details
Share where collusion is chosen (left chart)

Raising the preferenced volume share from 0 to 0.6 raises the share of cells where collusion is chosen from 0.26 to 0.33. Why: preferenced flow is shared whatever the price, so an undercut steals less volume and cheating pays less.

Raising the fine ratio from 0 to 500 lowers the share of cells where collusion is chosen from 0.67 to 0.05. Why: bigger fines raise the expected cost of being convicted.

Highest: 0.75 at φ = 0.6, fine ratio = 0; lowest: 0.04 at φ = 0, fine ratio = 500.

The two interact: going from the lowest to the highest φ changes the value by +0.15 when fine ratio = 0 but by +0.01 when fine ratio = 500.

Mechanism detail: The fine acts on participation (in tick-free cells collusion pays iff π_c(θ) > ρ_E(θ)·F at some θ) and φ on the incentive constraint, so φ's proportional gain is similar at every fine (φ 0 → 0.6: +25% at no fine, +20% at 500×). At zero fine the tick-free shares (0.644, 0.733, 0.844 by φ) are the pure sustainability frontier: there, collusion happens whenever any θ > 0 satisfies the incentive constraint.

Chosen collusion intensity θ* (right chart)

Raising the preferenced volume share from 0 to 0.6 raises the mean collusion intensity θ* from 0.17 to 0.24. Why: preferenced flow is shared whatever the price, so an undercut steals less volume and cheating pays less.

Raising the fine ratio from 0 to 500 lowers the mean collusion intensity θ* from 0.45 to 0.04. Why: bigger fines raise the expected cost of being convicted.

Highest: 0.53 at φ = 0.6, fine ratio = 0; lowest: 0.04 at φ = 0, fine ratio = 500.

The two interact: going from the lowest to the highest φ changes the value by +0.16 when fine ratio = 0 but by +0.01 when fine ratio = 500.

Mechanism detail: θ* falls with the fine at every φ (φ = 0.6: 0.533 → 0.046); φ's absolute advantage shrinks from 0.160 at no fine to 0.009 at 500×. That is because heavy fines leave few colluders, and most of those are hazard-free cells (no screen, no tip: 68% of colluders at 500×), where φ has little left to change.
