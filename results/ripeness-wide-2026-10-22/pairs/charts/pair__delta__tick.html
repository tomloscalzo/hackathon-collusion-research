Interpretation

Whether firms collude: It appears that a larger tick size (the minimum price step) generally lowers the share of colluding scenarios, while patience (the chance the market continues another round) raises it up to fairly high levels before it eases off. The tick size cuts both ways: among impatient firms a moderate price step actually helps the cartel, most likely because undercutting by a whole step is a big cut when margins are thin, and the price war drops below what firms earn in normal competition with a price step. Among very patient firms the markets with a price step fall away while markets without a price step keep rising. That late fall appears to be a modelling simplification in markets with a minimum price step (an investigation that finds nothing is treated as worse for firms than a conviction), not a real effect of patience: it disappears when every investigation ends in a conviction.

How hard they collude: It appears that collusion intensity (how far the chosen spread sits between the competitive and the monopoly level) rises then falls with patience (the chance the market continues another round), and that a larger tick size (the minimum price step) lowers it at most patience levels. Without a price step, intensity keeps rising until very high patience and then eases only a little, which seems to be genuine caution by cartels protecting their future profits. With a large price step it peaks earlier and drops much more, which appears to be mostly a modelling simplification in markets with a minimum price step (an investigation that finds nothing is treated as worse for firms than a conviction). A price step also most likely forces the cartel to move in whole steps, so it cannot fine-tune a modest, less conspicuous price.

Details
Share where collusion is chosen (left chart)

The share of cells where collusion is chosen peaks at patience = 0.9 (0.37) and is lower at both ends (0.17 at 0.5, 0.27 at 0.99). Why: patience helps up to a point (future cartel profits are worth protecting and the price-war threat bites); the decline at very high patience comes from the π_post = 0 calibration in tick markets (see Analysis).

Raising the tick size from 0 to 0.5 lowers the share of cells where collusion is chosen from 0.38 to 0.20. Why: a coarser tick gives competitors a profit floor (tick rents), so colluding gains less over competing; in this calibration it also makes an inconclusive investigation costly (π_post = 0).

Highest: 0.51 at δ = 0.99, tick = 0; lowest: 0.11 at δ = 0.99, tick = 0.5.

The two interact: going from the lowest to the highest δ changes the value by +0.39 when tick = 0 but by -0.05 when tick = 0.5.

Caveat: patterns involving tick, patience or P(evidence) are partly shaped by the π_post = 0 calibration in tick markets (a recorded limitation).

Mechanism detail: The tick pulls two ways. At low patience it helps (δ = 0.5: 0.231 with a 0.25 tick vs 0.115 without): a one-tick undercut is a large proportional cut at narrow margins and the price war falls to s₀, below the tick-rent Nash margin, so cheating is less tempting; with no screen and no tip the δ = 0.5 share is 0.560 with a 0.25 tick vs 0.267 without. At high patience the tick columns fall (tick 0.25: 0.380 at δ = 0.9 to 0.188 at 0.99) while the no-tick column keeps rising to 0.510; this decline is the π_post = 0 artifact, because restricting to P(evidence) = 1, where every trigger ends in conviction, the tick-0.25 share is flat (0.290 at δ = 0.9, 0.293 at 0.99).

Chosen collusion intensity θ* (right chart)

The mean collusion intensity θ* peaks at patience = 0.9 (0.26) and is lower at both ends (0.10 at 0.5, 0.17 at 0.99). Why: patience helps up to a point (future cartel profits are worth protecting and the price-war threat bites), but very patient coalitions choose lower margins to avoid the screen, and in tick markets the π_post = 0 calibration makes long horizons costly.

Raising the tick size from 0 to 0.5 lowers the mean collusion intensity θ* from 0.26 to 0.14. Why: a coarser tick gives competitors a profit floor (tick rents), so colluding gains less over competing; in this calibration it also makes an inconclusive investigation costly (π_post = 0).

Highest: 0.34 at δ = 0.95, tick = 0; lowest: 0.08 at δ = 0.5, tick = 0.

The two interact: going from the lowest to the highest δ changes the value by +0.22 when tick = 0 but by -0.01 when tick = 0.5.

Caveat: patterns involving tick, patience or P(evidence) are partly shaped by the π_post = 0 calibration in tick markets (a recorded limitation).

Mechanism detail: Tick-free θ* rises to 0.343 at δ = 0.95 and eases to 0.300 at 0.99 (genuine external-risk restraint), whereas with a 0.5 tick it peaks at 0.182 (δ = 0.8) and falls to 0.084. At P(evidence) = 1 the 0.25-tick column is nearly flat at high δ (0.222 → 0.202), so most of the high-δ collapse is the calibration, not economics. Coarser ticks lower θ* at every δ ≥ 0.8, plausibly because the cartel must move in whole-tick steps and cannot fine-tune a low-visibility margin.
