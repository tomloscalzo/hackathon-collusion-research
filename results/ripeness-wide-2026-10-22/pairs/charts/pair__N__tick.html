Interpretation

Whether firms collude: It appears that collusion falls as the number of market makers (competing firms) and the tick size (the minimum price step) grow, but a larger price step hurts much less when there are many market makers. A price step seems to bring an offsetting benefit there: undercutting by a whole step is a big cut when each firm's margin is thin, and the price war falls below normal earnings in markets with a price step, so more large cartels can hold together. With very few market makers only the costs show: fewer prices to choose from, a minimum markup that the regulator's screen (its rule for opening investigations when spreads are wide) can always see, and a modelling simplification in markets with a minimum price step (an investigation that finds nothing is treated as worse for firms than a conviction).

How hard they collude: It appears that collusion intensity (how far the chosen spread sits between the competitive and the monopoly level) falls as the number of market makers (competing firms) grows and as the tick size (the minimum price step) grows, with the price step cutting intensity by a similar proportion at every number of market makers, slightly less when there are many. The fall with tick size most likely reflects having to move prices in whole steps, the regulator's screen (its rule for opening investigations when spreads are wide) always seeing at least a full step of markup, and a modelling simplification in markets with a minimum price step (an investigation that finds nothing is treated as worse for firms than a conviction). The slightly smaller cut with many market makers is consistent with the price step making cheating less tempting there.

Details
Share where collusion is chosen (left chart)

Raising the number of market makers from 2 to 10 lowers the share of cells where collusion is chosen from 0.35 to 0.19. Why: each member's share of the cartel profit is 1/N, while a one-round undercut still captures most of the contestable flow, so the temptation to cheat grows with N.

Raising the tick size from 0 to 0.5 lowers the share of cells where collusion is chosen from 0.38 to 0.20. Why: a coarser tick gives competitors a profit floor (tick rents), so colluding gains less over competing; in this calibration it also makes an inconclusive investigation costly (π_post = 0).

Highest: 0.49 at N = 2, tick = 0; lowest: 0.13 at N = 10, tick = 0.5.

The two interact: going from the lowest to the highest N changes the value by -0.25 when tick = 0 but by -0.09 when tick = 0.5.

Caveat: patterns involving tick, patience or P(evidence) are partly shaped by the π_post = 0 calibration in tick markets (a recorded limitation).

Mechanism detail: A tick lowers the share at every N but by less at large N (N = 2: 0.494 → 0.216; N = 10: 0.247 → 0.129), because it has an offsetting benefit that matters only where the incentive constraint binds: with a 0.25 tick a one-tick undercut is a large proportional cut and the price war drops below the tick-rent margin, so the share of N = 10 cells where some collusion is sustainable rises from 0.465 to 0.628. At N = 2, where the constraint rarely binds, only the costs show: fewer price steps, a minimum markup of one full tick for the screen to see, and the π_post = 0 artifact. With P(evidence) = 1 (artifact off), a 0.25 tick even nudges the share up at N = 10 (0.177 → 0.183) while still cutting it at N = 2 (0.349 → 0.279).

Chosen collusion intensity θ* (right chart)

Raising the number of market makers from 2 to 10 lowers the mean collusion intensity θ* from 0.27 to 0.12. Why: each member's share of the cartel profit is 1/N, while a one-round undercut still captures most of the contestable flow, so the temptation to cheat grows with N.

Raising the tick size from 0 to 0.5 lowers the mean collusion intensity θ* from 0.26 to 0.14. Why: a coarser tick gives competitors a profit floor (tick rents), so colluding gains less over competing; in this calibration it also makes an inconclusive investigation costly (π_post = 0).

Highest: 0.35 at N = 2, tick = 0; lowest: 0.08 at N = 10, tick = 0.5.

The two interact: going from the lowest to the highest N changes the value by -0.21 when tick = 0 but by -0.10 when tick = 0.5.

Caveat: patterns involving tick, patience or P(evidence) are partly shaped by the π_post = 0 calibration in tick markets (a recorded limitation).

Mechanism detail: θ* falls with the tick at every N (N = 2: 0.355 → 0.176; N = 10: 0.145 → 0.079), by about half in each case, slightly less at N = 10 (−46% vs −50%), consistent with the tick's easing of the incentive constraint there. The fall reflects whole-tick steps, the screen seeing at least one tick of markup, and the tick artifact.
