Interpretation

Whether firms collude: It appears that a higher fine ratio (the fine as a multiple of a firm's full-collusion profit for a round) steadily reduces collusion at every level of patience (the chance the market continues another round), as long as the regulator's screen (its rule for opening investigations when spreads are wide) or tips (investigations that start at random, whatever the price) can start an investigation. Patience raises the share at every fine ratio up to fairly high values, most likely because the threat of a price war holds patient cartels together. When the fine ratio is small, the share drops again among the most patient firms, which appears to be a modelling simplification in markets with a minimum price step (an investigation that finds nothing is treated as worse for firms than a conviction) rather than an effect of patience itself.

How hard they collude: It appears that collusion intensity (how far the chosen spread sits between the competitive and the monopoly level) falls as the fine ratio (the fine as a multiple of a firm's full-collusion profit for a round) rises at every level of patience (the chance the market continues another round), most likely because each extra bit of price raises the chance of an investigation and therefore the expected fine. Intensity rises then falls with patience at every fine ratio. Under very high fine ratios the patience levels converge on low intensity, suggesting that once fines are severe, patience makes little difference to how far prices are pushed.

Details
Share where collusion is chosen (left chart)

The share of cells where collusion is chosen peaks at patience = 0.9 (0.37) and is lower at both ends (0.17 at 0.5, 0.27 at 0.99). Why: patience helps up to a point (future cartel profits are worth protecting and the price-war threat bites); the decline at very high patience comes from the π_post = 0 calibration in tick markets (see Analysis).

Raising the fine ratio from 0 to 500 lowers the share of cells where collusion is chosen from 0.67 to 0.05. Why: bigger fines raise the expected cost of being convicted.

Highest: 0.85 at δ = 0.9, fine ratio = 0; lowest: 0.02 at δ = 0.5, fine ratio = 500.

The two interact: going from the lowest to the highest δ changes the value by +0.17 when fine ratio = 0 but by +0.03 when fine ratio = 500.

Caveat: patterns involving tick, patience or P(evidence) are partly shaped by the π_post = 0 calibration in tick markets (a recorded limitation).

Mechanism detail: fineRatio is F as a multiple of one round of full-monopoly profit per firm (π_c at θ = 1); the fine bites only where a screen or tip can trigger, and the share falls steadily (δ = 0.9: 0.848 with no fine to 0.059 at 500×). Patience raises the share in every fine column up to δ = 0.9 by easing the incentive constraint; beyond that, in tick-free markets participation does not depend on δ (the gain has the sign of π_c − ρ_E·F), so the tick-free share at zero fine is 1.000 at both δ = 0.95 and 0.99. The δ = 0.99 drop at low fines (zero fine: 0.848 at δ = 0.9 to 0.580) comes from tick cells (0.805 → 0.370), the π_post = 0 artifact.

Chosen collusion intensity θ* (right chart)

The mean collusion intensity θ* peaks at patience = 0.9 (0.26) and is lower at both ends (0.10 at 0.5, 0.17 at 0.99). Why: patience helps up to a point (future cartel profits are worth protecting and the price-war threat bites), but very patient coalitions choose lower margins to avoid the screen, and in tick markets the π_post = 0 calibration makes long horizons costly.

Raising the fine ratio from 0 to 500 lowers the mean collusion intensity θ* from 0.45 to 0.04. Why: bigger fines raise the expected cost of being convicted.

Highest: 0.59 at δ = 0.9, fine ratio = 0; lowest: 0.02 at δ = 0.5, fine ratio = 500.

Caveat: patterns involving tick, patience or P(evidence) are partly shaped by the π_post = 0 calibration in tick markets (a recorded limitation).

Mechanism detail: θ* falls with the fine in every row (δ = 0.9: 0.591 → 0.052) because the marginal penalty of extra markup, slope × P(evidence) × F, scales with F. Beyond the incentive constraint the two barely interact: every fine column peaks at δ = 0.9 (tied with 0.95 at 250×), and at 500× the rows converge (0.017–0.052).
