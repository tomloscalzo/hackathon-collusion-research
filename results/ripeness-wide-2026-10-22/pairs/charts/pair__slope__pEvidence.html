Interpretation

Whether firms collude: It appears that collusion falls as the regulator screen slope (how strongly the regulator's screen, its rule for opening investigations, reacts to wide spreads) and P(evidence | investigation) (the chance an investigation produces a guilty finding) rise, and that these reinforce each other: a higher chance of a guilty finding matters much more under a steeper screen. This is most likely because the screen creates investigations while the chance of a guilty finding decides how costly each investigation is. The small upturn when a guilty finding is certain under steep screens appears to be a modelling simplification in markets with a minimum price step (an investigation that finds nothing is treated as worse for firms than a conviction); in markets with no price step, collusion falls steadily.

How hard they collude: It appears that collusion intensity (how far the chosen spread sits between the competitive and the monopoly level) falls with the regulator screen slope (how strongly the regulator's screen, its rule for opening investigations, reacts to wide spreads) and with P(evidence | investigation) (the chance an investigation produces a guilty finding), at every level of each. A higher chance of a guilty finding most likely raises the expected fine attached to each extra bit of price. The slight upturns when a guilty finding is certain under steep screens appear to be a modelling simplification in markets with a minimum price step (an investigation that finds nothing is treated as worse for firms than a conviction) rather than a real effect.

Details
Share where collusion is chosen (left chart)

Raising the screen slope from 0 to 0.2 lowers the share of cells where collusion is chosen from 0.55 to 0.14. Why: a steeper screen makes high spreads more likely to trigger an investigation, so cartels must keep margins low or not collude.

Raising the probability an investigation finds evidence from 0.1 to 1 lowers the share of cells where collusion is chosen from 0.39 to 0.23. Why: a higher chance that an investigation ends in a conviction makes the fine more likely to be paid.

Highest: 0.64 at slope = 0, P(evidence) = 0.1; lowest: 0.11 at slope = 0.2, P(evidence) = 0.6.

Caveat: patterns involving tick, patience or P(evidence) are partly shaped by the π_post = 0 calibration in tick markets (a recorded limitation).

Mechanism detail: P(evidence) prices each trigger and the screen generates triggers, so the two compound: the proportional fall from P(evidence) 0.1 to 1.0 is 24% at slope 0 (0.636 → 0.485), where only tips trigger, and 46% at slope 0.05 (0.384 → 0.207). The upturn at P(evidence) = 1 for slopes ≥ 0.15 (slope 0.2: 0.109 → 0.115) is the tick artifact: tick-free shares fall monotonically (slope 0.2: 0.385 → 0.141), while tick cells rise between P(evidence) 0.6 and 1.0 (0.075 → 0.101).

Chosen collusion intensity θ* (right chart)

Raising the screen slope from 0 to 0.2 lowers the mean collusion intensity θ* from 0.53 to 0.06. Why: a steeper screen makes high spreads more likely to trigger an investigation, so cartels must keep margins low or not collude.

Raising the probability an investigation finds evidence from 0.1 to 1 lowers the mean collusion intensity θ* from 0.26 to 0.17. Why: a higher chance that an investigation ends in a conviction makes the fine more likely to be paid.

Highest: 0.60 at slope = 0, P(evidence) = 0.1; lowest: 0.05 at slope = 0.2, P(evidence) = 0.6.

The two interact: going from the lowest to the highest slope changes the value by -0.52 when P(evidence) = 0.1 but by -0.41 when P(evidence) = 1.

Caveat: patterns involving tick, patience or P(evidence) are partly shaped by the π_post = 0 calibration in tick markets (a recorded limitation).

Mechanism detail: θ* falls with P(evidence) at every slope (0.602 → 0.463 at slope 0; 0.086 → 0.054 at 0.2). The small upturns at P(evidence) = 1 for slopes ≥ 0.15 (0.061 → 0.065 at 0.15; 0.048 → 0.054 at 0.2) are the same artifact.
