Interpretation

Whether firms collude: It appears that collusion falls sharply as the regulator screen slope (how strongly the regulator's screen, its rule for opening investigations, reacts to wide spreads) rises, and rises with the preferenced volume share (the share of orders split evenly between firms whatever their price), which lifts the whole curve by a broadly similar proportion. This seems to be because they do different jobs: the preferenced volume share decides whether a cartel can be held together, while the screen decides whether colluding is worth the risk of investigation. Moving up the chart therefore shows easier cartel discipline, and moving across shows enforcement.

How hard they collude: It appears that collusion intensity (how far the chosen spread sits between the competitive and the monopoly level) falls steeply as the regulator screen slope (how strongly the regulator's screen, its rule for opening investigations, reacts to wide spreads) rises, and rises with the preferenced volume share (the share of orders split evenly between firms whatever their price) at every screen slope. The proportional boost from the preferenced volume share seems larger under steep screens, most likely because a steep screen makes high prices risky, which in turn makes cheating more tempting, and evenly split volume relieves exactly that temptation.

Details
Share where collusion is chosen (left chart)

Raising the preferenced volume share from 0 to 0.6 raises the share of cells where collusion is chosen from 0.26 to 0.33. Why: preferenced flow is shared whatever the price, so an undercut steals less volume and cheating pays less.

Raising the screen slope from 0 to 0.2 lowers the share of cells where collusion is chosen from 0.55 to 0.14. Why: a steeper screen makes high spreads more likely to trigger an investigation, so cartels must keep margins low or not collude.

Highest: 0.61 at φ = 0.6, slope = 0; lowest: 0.13 at φ = 0, slope = 0.2.

Mechanism detail: φ lifts the whole screen curve by 22–31% (φ 0 → 0.6: 0.500 → 0.609 at slope 0; 0.125 → 0.164 at 0.2), since φ works only on the incentive constraint and the screen mainly on participation. The share of cells where collusion is sustainable barely depends on the slope (φ = 0: 0.693 → 0.653) but strongly on φ (0.693 → 0.853 at slope 0), so the vertical gaps are sustainability gains and the horizontal decline is enforcement.

Chosen collusion intensity θ* (right chart)

Raising the preferenced volume share from 0 to 0.6 raises the mean collusion intensity θ* from 0.17 to 0.24. Why: preferenced flow is shared whatever the price, so an undercut steals less volume and cheating pays less.

Raising the screen slope from 0 to 0.2 lowers the mean collusion intensity θ* from 0.53 to 0.06. Why: a steeper screen makes high spreads more likely to trigger an investigation, so cartels must keep margins low or not collude.

Highest: 0.59 at φ = 0.6, slope = 0; lowest: 0.05 at φ = 0, slope = 0.2.

Mechanism detail: θ* falls steeply with the slope (φ = 0: 0.471 → 0.048), and φ raises it in every column, from +25% at slope 0 (0.471 → 0.587) to +60% at 0.2 (0.048 → 0.077). The proportional φ gain likely grows with the screen because a steeper screen raises the hazard at high margins and so tightens the incentive constraint there, which φ relaxes.
