Interpretation

Whether firms collude: It appears that collusion rises with the preferenced volume share (the share of orders split evenly between firms whatever their price) and falls with the tick size (the minimum price step). The preferenced volume share helps at every tick size but less when there is a price step, most likely because the step already eases the temptation to cheat, leaving less for the preferenced volume share to do. In very small markets with a price step, a higher preferenced volume share also raises ordinary competitive earnings, which seems to shrink the space for a cartel and can even lower collusion.

How hard they collude: It appears that collusion intensity (how far the chosen spread sits between the competitive and the monopoly level) rises with the preferenced volume share (the share of orders split evenly between firms whatever their price) and falls with the tick size (the minimum price step), with these effects adding up almost independently. In markets with no price step, the preferenced volume share does not change prices, the regulator's screen (its rule for opening investigations when spreads are wide) or the fine, so its effect most likely comes entirely from easing the temptation to cheat, letting more cartels form and fewer hold prices down to stay together. The price step appears to lower intensity by forcing whole steps that the screen can see.

Details
Share where collusion is chosen (left chart)

Raising the preferenced volume share from 0 to 0.6 raises the share of cells where collusion is chosen from 0.26 to 0.33. Why: preferenced flow is shared whatever the price, so an undercut steals less volume and cheating pays less.

Raising the tick size from 0 to 0.5 lowers the share of cells where collusion is chosen from 0.38 to 0.20. Why: a coarser tick gives competitors a profit floor (tick rents), so colluding gains less over competing; in this calibration it also makes an inconclusive investigation costly (π_post = 0).

Highest: 0.43 at φ = 0.6, tick = 0; lowest: 0.18 at φ = 0, tick = 0.5.

Caveat: patterns involving tick, patience or P(evidence) are partly shaped by the π_post = 0 calibration in tick markets (a recorded limitation).

Mechanism detail: φ eases the incentive constraint; the tick removes room and brings the artifact. φ helps in every tick column (tick 0: 0.329 → 0.433; tick 0.5: 0.181 → 0.217), but less with a tick, partly because the tick already eases the constraint (at φ = 0, collusion is sustainable in 0.790 of cells with a 0.25 tick vs 0.625 without), leaving less for φ to do, and partly because at N = 2 φ raises the tick-rent Nash margin: in N = 2 cells with a 0.25 tick the share falls with φ (0.375 → 0.310).

Chosen collusion intensity θ* (right chart)

Raising the preferenced volume share from 0 to 0.6 raises the mean collusion intensity θ* from 0.17 to 0.24. Why: preferenced flow is shared whatever the price, so an undercut steals less volume and cheating pays less.

Raising the tick size from 0 to 0.5 lowers the mean collusion intensity θ* from 0.26 to 0.14. Why: a coarser tick gives competitors a profit floor (tick rents), so colluding gains less over competing; in this calibration it also makes an inconclusive investigation costly (π_post = 0).

Highest: 0.30 at φ = 0.6, tick = 0; lowest: 0.12 at φ = 0, tick = 0.5.

Caveat: patterns involving tick, patience or P(evidence) are partly shaped by the π_post = 0 calibration in tick markets (a recorded limitation).

Mechanism detail: θ* rises with φ (tick 0: 0.213 → 0.305) and falls with the tick (φ = 0: 0.213 → 0.123), almost additively. In tick-free markets φ does not touch prices, the screen or the fine, so its effect on θ* runs entirely through the incentive constraint: more cells collude and fewer are capped below their preferred margin.
