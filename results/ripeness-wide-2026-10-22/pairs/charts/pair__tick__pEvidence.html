Interpretation

Whether firms collude: It appears that collusion falls with the tick size (the minimum price step) and, on average, as P(evidence | investigation) (the chance an investigation produces a guilty finding) rises. In markets with no price step, a higher chance of a guilty finding acts as pure deterrence, because it raises the expected fine, so collusion falls steadily. In markets with a price step the curve flattens and even turns up when a guilty finding is certain, which appears to be a modelling simplification for such markets (an investigation that finds nothing is treated as worse for firms than a conviction): an investigation that finds nothing ends ordinary earnings for good, while a conviction only costs the fine, so a higher chance of a guilty finding looks less bad.

How hard they collude: It appears that collusion intensity (how far the chosen spread sits between the competitive and the monopoly level) falls as the tick size (the minimum price step) grows and as P(evidence | investigation) (the chance an investigation produces a guilty finding) rises. In markets with no price step, intensity falls steadily as a guilty finding becomes more likely, most likely because only the expected fine, the chance of conviction combined with the fine, matters. With a large price step it bottoms out and rises slightly when a guilty finding is certain, which seems to be a modelling simplification in markets with a minimum price step (an investigation that finds nothing is treated as worse for firms than a conviction).

Details
Share where collusion is chosen (left chart)

Raising the tick size from 0 to 0.5 lowers the share of cells where collusion is chosen from 0.38 to 0.20. Why: a coarser tick gives competitors a profit floor (tick rents), so colluding gains less over competing; in this calibration it also makes an inconclusive investigation costly (π_post = 0).

Raising the probability an investigation finds evidence from 0.1 to 1 lowers the share of cells where collusion is chosen from 0.39 to 0.23. Why: a higher chance that an investigation ends in a conviction makes the fine more likely to be paid.

Highest: 0.52 at tick = 0, P(evidence) = 0.1; lowest: 0.17 at tick = 0.5, P(evidence) = 0.6.

The two interact: going from the lowest to the highest tick changes the value by -0.27 when P(evidence) = 0.1 but by -0.09 when P(evidence) = 1.

Caveat: patterns involving tick, patience or P(evidence) are partly shaped by the π_post = 0 calibration in tick markets (a recorded limitation).

Mechanism detail: Without a tick P(evidence) is pure deterrence: it multiplies the fine, and the share falls from 0.523 to 0.269. With a tick the curve flattens and turns up at 1.0 (tick 0.5: 0.170 → 0.176), and at zero fine the pooled tick share rises with P(evidence) (0.555 → 0.780) while the tick-free share stays at 0.741. That is the π_post = 0 artifact: a trigger without evidence costs the tick rents forever, a conviction costs F and returns firms to π^N, so more evidence is less bad when fines are small.

Chosen collusion intensity θ* (right chart)

Raising the tick size from 0 to 0.5 lowers the mean collusion intensity θ* from 0.26 to 0.14. Why: a coarser tick gives competitors a profit floor (tick rents), so colluding gains less over competing; in this calibration it also makes an inconclusive investigation costly (π_post = 0).

Raising the probability an investigation finds evidence from 0.1 to 1 lowers the mean collusion intensity θ* from 0.26 to 0.17. Why: a higher chance that an investigation ends in a conviction makes the fine more likely to be paid.

Highest: 0.35 at tick = 0, P(evidence) = 0.1; lowest: 0.12 at tick = 0.5, P(evidence) = 0.6.

The two interact: going from the lowest to the highest tick changes the value by -0.18 when P(evidence) = 0.1 but by -0.06 when P(evidence) = 1.

Caveat: patterns involving tick, patience or P(evidence) are partly shaped by the π_post = 0 calibration in tick markets (a recorded limitation).

Mechanism detail: Tick-free θ* falls from 0.350 to 0.187 with P(evidence), but with a 0.5 tick it bottoms at 0.6 (0.123) and rises at 1.0 (0.128), the same artifact. Without a tick the expected fine depends only on the product P(evidence)×F: θ* at P(evidence) = 0.1 with fine 125 equals θ* at 1.0 with fine 12.5 (0.264).
